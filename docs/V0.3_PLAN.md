### Confirmed defects

- **B1 — No match start.** Combat is live from the first tick; "FIGHT" is shown while both fighters can already act, so the AI and the player can move during the intro.
- **B2 — Blocking always auto-turns.** `_tick_block` calls `face_opponent()` every tick, so a jump cross-up is always blocked. The "attack from behind" check in `receive_hit` can never trigger.
- **B3 — Premature result.** The result text appears a fixed 50 ticks after the finisher's beats end. The loser can still be flying, rolling or settling at that point.
- **B4 — No facing-left coverage.** Hitbox mirroring is correct in code, but no test checks attacks while facing left.

- **B5 — Bodies overlapped.** The pushbox kept the fighters' centres only 44 px
  apart, which is less than a silhouette's width. It is now 60 px, and
  `test_match` asserts it. Strike poses still visually overlap at point-blank
  range; that is M4/M5 work.

### Tuning and missing items (later phases)

These are not defects: parry, posture, explicit combo branches, back kick, close-range variants, AI archetypes, progressive crack growth with debris, a training mode, and a pre-match flow.

## 3. Input priority

**Neutral (IDLE/WALK).** These are checked in order, once per tick:

1. **Attack.** A buffered attack wins over everything and turns the fighter to face the opponent.
2. **Dodge.** A double-tap buffer.
3. **Jump.** A buffer.
4. **Block.** Shift held. With S held as well, it is a low block. A fresh press, within the 4-tick parry buffer and outside the cooldown, opens the parry window.
5. **Crouch.** S held.
6. **Walk.** A or D.

**CROUCH:** attack, then dodge, then jump, then block, then stand.

**BLOCK:** an attack, a jump, or releasing Shift leaves through GUARD_RELEASE. That takes 6 ticks, or 14 after a whiffed parry. A buffered attack or jump survives the release.

**PARRY (deflect):** attack, then dodge, then jump. Otherwise, after 8 ticks the fighter returns to BLOCK if Shift is still held, or to IDLE if it isn't.

**DODGE:** an attack pressed during the dodge is held and fires when the dodge ends. On a perfect dodge it fires at the end of the i-frames.

**Hit resolution order** (per hit):
1. Dodge i-frames: whiff, and it counts as a perfect dodge.
2. Parry window, facing the attacker, at the right height: PARRIED.
3. Raised guard, facing the attacker, at the right height: BLOCKED.
4. Otherwise: HIT, or KO.

## 4. Future grappling (design note only, nothing implemented)

`FinisherDirector` already proves the needed pattern. It is a two-fighter director that:
- owns both fighters through `begin_scripted` / `end_scripted`,
- drives named poses and relative motion per beat,
- leaves gravity and landing to `Fighter.tick()`.

A future `InteractionDirector` would generalise it with:

- `role` (initiator / receiver) on each fighter, plus an `interaction_id` so only one interaction runs at a time.
- An attachment anchor, such as a joint index on the initiator, that the receiver's root follows each tick.
- A trajectory curve for throws: ballistic hand-off to `Fighter.blowback()`.
- Break conditions checked per beat: hit taken, escape input within a window, or posture.

No new states are needed beyond reusing FINISHER/FINISHED, or one GRAPPLED state later.
